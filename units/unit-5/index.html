Key vocabulary

BondA loan you make to a government or company. They pay you interest and pay back the loan later.
IssuerWho borrows the money: the U.S. Treasury, a state or city, or a company.
Face (par) valueThe amount repaid at maturity, usually $1,000.
CouponThe bond's interest rate. Yearly interest = face value × coupon rate.
MaturityThe date the issuer pays back the face value.
Treasury / TIPSBonds backed by the U.S. government. TIPS adjust for inflation.
Municipal bondIssued by a state, city or school district. Interest is usually free of federal income tax.
Corporate bondIssued by a company. Pays more than a Treasury because it is riskier.
Current yieldYearly interest ÷ the bond's price today.
Interest-rate riskRates up → existing bond prices down (and the reverse). The seesaw.
Credit ratingA grade for default risk. BBB or higher = investment grade; BB or lower = junk (high-yield).
Yield curveYields from short to long maturities. Normal: long rates higher. Inverted: short rates higher.
Bond fund / ETFA fund holding many bonds. More diversified, but no maturity date.
Bank runMany depositors pulling money at once, faster than a bank can pay without selling assets.
