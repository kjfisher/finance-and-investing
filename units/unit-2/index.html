Key vocabulary

ExchangeA marketplace where shares trade, such as the NYSE or Nasdaq.
TickerA company's short trading code, such as AAPL for Apple.
Bid / ask / spreadBid: the most a buyer will pay. Ask: the least a seller will take. Spread: ask − bid.
Market orderBuy or sell right now at the best available price.
Limit orderBuy or sell only at your price or better. It may never fill.
Stop orderBecomes a market order when the price hits your stop. Used to limit losses.
MarginBuying with borrowed money. Gains and losses both get bigger.
Short sellingSelling borrowed shares, hoping to buy them back cheaper. Losses have no upper limit.
Market capPrice × shares outstanding. Large > $10B, mid $2–$10B, small < $2B.
SectorA group of similar businesses. The S&P 500 uses 11 sectors.
IndexA basket that tracks part of the market: the Dow (30 companies), S&P 500 (about 500), Nasdaq Composite (tech-heavy).
Bull / bear marketBull: a long rise. Bear: a drop of 20% or more.
CorrectionA drop of 10% or more from a recent high.
BubblePrices pushed far above what businesses are worth by excitement and fear of missing out.
