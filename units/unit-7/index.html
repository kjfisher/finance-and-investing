Key vocabulary

DerivativeA contract whose value comes from something else: a stock, oil, wheat, an index.
CallThe right (not the duty) to BUY at the strike price before expiration.
PutThe right (not the duty) to SELL at the strike price before expiration.
Strike priceThe price the option lets you buy or sell at.
PremiumWhat the option costs. 1 contract = 100 shares, so cost = premium × 100.
ExpirationThe last day the option can be used.
Call profit at expiration(Stock − strike) − premium per share. Below the strike you lose the premium. Breakeven = strike + premium.
Put profit at expiration(Strike − stock) − premium per share.
Covered callOwn the shares + sell a call: earn income, give up gains above the strike.
Protective putOwn the shares + buy a put: insurance against a drop; the premium is the cost.
Futures contractA DUTY to buy or sell a set amount at a set price on a future date.
Hedger vs. speculatorHedgers reduce a risk they already have; speculators bet on a price move.
LeverageA small deposit controls a big contract, so small price moves mean big gains or losses.
Short squeezeShort sellers rush to buy back shares as the price rises, pushing it even higher.
