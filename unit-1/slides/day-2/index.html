Compound interest · Notes box 2

$1,000 at 10% a year

After year 1$1,000 × 1.10 = $1,100
After year 2$1,100 × 1.10 = $1,210
After year 3$1,210 × 1.10 = $1,331
Simple interest (no compounding)$1,000 + 3 × $100 = $1,300

Time does the heavy lifting

$10,000 at 8% a year for 36 years

Compounded once a year; no money added

Compounding turns $10,000 into about $159,700. Simple interest would reach only $38,800.

Rule of 72 · Notes box 3

Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate

At 6%72 ÷ 6 = 12 years
At 9%72 ÷ 9 = 8 years
At 12%72 ÷ 12 = 6 years
Bell ringer: 8% for 36 years9 years to double · 4 doubles · about $16,000

Inflation · Notes box 4

Prices rise, so money has to grow faster

  • Inflation: prices rising over time
  • CPI (Consumer Price Index): the price of a basket of things people buy
  • GDP: the value of everything the country produces (more on Days 3–4)
Inflation · Notes box 4

What inflation does to $100

Calculated: $100 ÷ 1.03 each year

At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years (72 ÷ 3), so $100 buys about half as much.
